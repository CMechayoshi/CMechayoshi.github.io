Nintendo Adventure Book # 8: Flown the Koopa

Intro: In this book written by Matt Wayne, Mario and the crew visit Dinosaur Land for the Dino-Flying Derby. Soon however something is amiss, and a Magikoopa is behind it! Use the puzzles to stop this crafty Koopa menace! This is adapted from a handwritten draft from 10/7/19. Since I don’t own this book at the moment, I used images (book has since been properly archived) to create this guide. As usual, look on the Nintendo Adventure Books Key to understand how to use this page.

The guide was created by Amia J. Moore/ C. Mechayoshi. It may not be copied without permission. Credits to that 'magichotdog' guy for scans.

Summary and conclusions:

As this was yet another in the series I never got to grow up with as a kid, my feelings could have went either way. I like the quirky Magikoopa cousin of Bowser. The Dinosaur Land settings are used well and memorable. There’s only a few major locations but they work. Writing and character interactions were some of the best in the series with lots of funny moments and banter, even amusing endings. Structure wise it gets linear in the very end, but I like the many paths you can take to get there. As long as you obtain the correct items you’ll make it. I just realized that they never explain who messed up the Dinosaur Derby. I mean it’s implied to be Bowser or the Magikoopa but there’s it's never cleared up...

Let’s touch on the items and puzzles. The early cookie bag puzzle somewhat uniquely (but in a bad way) has a quirk where what you chose means you do NOT get that item. This book has an ‘item of doom’, the flashlight which will screw you later in the book. They also under-emphasize what you need, so you couldn't get stuck while doing it all right. Overall the item checks are a little harsh, not the worse offenders in the family, but eager to penalize you too on hazard pages. While some puzzles are okay and well suited for the theme, others feel needlessly over complicated, tempting you to guess on choices. Points wise, the coins are a little scarce in the book. I liked the detail however, of how you can 'sacrifice' a bit of your score (via the popcorn of page seven)to make a later part easier, or tough it out to keep the higher score. The only other quirk I must point out is that I’m not sure how to max out the score. See the optimal path below. It's not a typical plot, and the structuring clearly had some thought and planning behind it, plus I like the Mario Bros action here. 4/5.

Optimal Path:
START: 1 (tickets, ten coins)- 93 (flower, feather)- 37- 46 (fifteen coins)- 113- 55- 118- 63- 51- 100 (ten coins, remote)- 22- 50- 115- 54- 80- 110- 90- 76- 31- 48 (ten coins)- 96- 85- 36- 77 (used flower)- 34- 58- 4 (use remote)- 61- 74 (three-hundred coins) END

This gives you 3450 points, “King of the Sewers” and the second highest. We skip the Monty Mole layer, because it goes really out of the way. Even with those points however, you won’t be a "Plumbersaurus Rex". This isn't exactly a loopy book and they rarely make the threshold so tight, so I'm leaning on score calculation error. By the way, no one else on the internet as far as I've seen got higher than 3500.

Puzzle analysis: Seventeen puzzles, most of them ‘logic’ type. There are two ‘mazes’, three ‘decipher’ puzzles, one maze/other, one word search, one decipher/logic, and one matching/logic. So logic wins? This isn’t the easiest of the books puzzle wise, but the difficultly is mostly having a lot of busy-work to do rather than needing to think hard.

Book goals:
-They want you to meet up with Bowser: Easy. You must to get the remote.

-Battle the thwomp: Is on the necessary path as well, so pointless to mention besides reinforcing that you’re on the right track.

-The Mega Mole layer: This can be easily skipped, but in their defense it does award coins. (Or might kill you.) It just felt so out of the way my optimal path skips it, but I totally understand the hint.
