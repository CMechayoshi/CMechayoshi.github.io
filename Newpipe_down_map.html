Nintendo Adventure Books #5: Pipe Down! Guide

Intro: This is the fifth book in the series by Clyde Bosco again. Mario and Luigi track down the princess after a pair of basketball shoes take her away from her own birthday party. This guide is adapted from a hand written draft I completed around 8/18/19. As usual, use F3 to navigate and look back on the portal page for how this works. Summary and conclusions:

This was the first Nintendo Adventure Book I’d purchased specifically to write a guide on, and I’m glad I did. The themes are a hodgepodge, music, dancing, shoes, and basketball? Bizarre, but it works. There aren’t a ton of areas to explore or enemy encounters, but the Mario bros play well off of each other and keep the pacing up. Many puzzles are based on music or sports.. and gears.. and logic. Very few are chance based. Pipe Down! understands the assignment when it comes to the CYOA thing. This is a structured adventure with plenty of detours, always leading back to common paths and no circular loops. My biggest gripe is how they handle the boots. Only at end of the princess chase (which turns out to be a shaggy dog route funnily enough) can you learn WHICH boots you need without guessing. You can use the wrong ones and survive, but say goodbye to the score.. Or that's what I'm sure they meant. In reality the threshold for the high score is surpassed just from reaching the last page, no matter how you did prior.

Otherwise items and pathing is given more than the usual attention for the series. You might get out of a situation more easily, without penalty, or get saved after making a mistake. Some bad paths pad it out a little before sending you to the game over. Villainous Ludwig Von Koopa has his personality and quirks incorporated into his master plan. It's no mystery he's behind it, but they try to foreshadow it anyway. He's menacing and brutal if you play badly, so don't. The bugged score is disappointing, but I loved this book regardless. Yeah I'm a bit biased too with the Ludwig part. So, Pipe Down! isn't perfect, but it was fun, well written, weird in the right ways, and decently structured. 5/5

Optimal Path:
Going for maximum score for a change: START Page 1 (ten coins, noisemaker)- 50- 96 (basketball)- 34- 103- 9 (one-hundred coins, Starman)- 73- 88 (five coins)- 36- 26 (cowboy boots)- 62- 114 (lose noisemaker)- 58- 92- 7- 39 (forty-two coins)- 28- 120- 110- 42- 55- 77- 56- 90- 76- 93 (four-hundred coins) END.

Your score will be five hundred seventy coins, for a score of five-thousand five hundred and seventy, ie: “Supercharged Koopa-Catching Power Plumber”.

Some explanations of my path:

  • The basketball is never used because we know which way to go at one point. Same for the Starman because we avoid the ‘worser’ path that requires it.
  • It is impossible to avoid a ‘buffet’ or penalty in this book. You will have to give the Clawgrip something on page one-hundred fourteen, whether it’s the otherwise useless party favors, boots, or money. (By the way, you can't hold all of the items at once like in some books. You'd need to reset at a point and wipe the inventory, and no matter what you can't reobtain the party favors you missed.)
  • We suspend the desert chase with the princess because we don't need the hint and it gives us no points. The score page hints encourages this however for obvious reasons.

Puzzle analysis: There are nineteen puzzles in the book. Five are ‘logic’, four ‘decipher’, two ‘get a ruler’, two ‘matching’, one 'decipher/math', one ‘word search’, 'one maze/logic', one ‘maze’, one ‘get a ruler’/decipher, one 'other' type puzzle. As mentioned before, the puzzles stick to themes well overall, but fall victim to a few issues. Some need some more clear instructions or other courtesies like a word bank. Other than that it’s solid.

Book goals:
-Did you visit the desert: This is how you actually track down Peach, not skip any story, and know what boots to use.

-Find the Starman: This item can save you on some pages, but our optimal path doesn’t need it because we make no mistakes. That’s all the hints we get.
